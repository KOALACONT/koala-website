Shepparton, VIC

Shipping containers Shepparton

Shepparton is flat, and people assume flat means easy. What flat actually means in the Goulburn Valley is that there is usually a channel, a drain or a farm bridge between the truck and the spot you have picked. The Goulburn–Murray network runs through orchards, dairy paddocks and the back of half the industrial blocks in town, and plenty of those crossings were built for a tractor rather than a loaded rigid. So the first question we ask about a Shepparton job is not where you want it. It is what we have to cross to get there.

Getting it onto the block

Start with the crossings. The irrigation network here is dense, and a lot of properties are reached over a private culvert or a narrow farm bridge whose load rating nobody has looked at since it went in. A tractor and a spray rig cross it every week without incident; a rigid truck carrying a container is a different proposition, and if it drops a wheel through the shoulder of a channel bank the day gets very long for everybody. If your access crosses a channel or a drain, tell us, and send a photo of the crossing from side on. Where the crossing is doubtful we will place the container from the nearest firm ground with a crane truck instead. Second, ground moisture behaves backwards here compared with most of the state. Irrigation means a paddock can be soft in February when everywhere else is like concrete, and a bay that has been watered in the last few days will not carry a truck no matter how good it looks. The soil across most of the valley is a grey duplex — a thin working layer over heavy clay — and once the clay is wet it turns greasy rather than boggy, which is worse, because a truck slides instead of sinking. Third, overhead. Orchard netting, hail structures, trellis wire, low sheds and the big old peppercorns along the older town streets all sit at exactly the height a tilt-tray bed wants when it is raised. And fourth, town itself. Central Shepparton and Mooroopna have older subdivisions with narrow drives and street trees, while Kialla, Shepparton North and Kialla Lakes have estate streets with kerbside service pits and parked cars either side. None of it is hard. All of it goes better if we know first.

Shepparton

Channels, culverts and the last hundred metres

In most places the tricky part of a delivery is the street. Around Shepparton it is the last hundred metres, and it is nearly always about water infrastructure. The channel and drain network runs everywhere, and it means the access to a lot of blocks is over a crossing that was designed for farm traffic. Channel banks look solid and are not: they are built ground, they hold moisture, and the shoulder is the first part to let go under a heavy wheel.

So we ask three things. Is there a crossing between the road and the placement spot, what does it look like side on, and has anything heavier than a tractor been over it recently. If the answer is unclear the safe play is a crane truck working from the nearest formed surface, which reaches over the problem entirely rather than driving across it. That is not a fallback, it is often the right first choice here, and deciding it at the quote costs nothing. Deciding it after a truck is bogged on a channel bank costs a great deal, and it is your channel and your neighbour's water that get affected.

On the ground

Orchard rows, netting and where a truck can turn

Orchard and vineyard deliveries have their own geometry. Rows are spaced for a tractor and a bin trailer, which means a rigid truck cannot swing a tilt bed inside them, and the only place with real turning space is the headland at the end of the run. On netted blocks the problem is vertical as well: hail netting, trellis wire and the support cables sit right where a raised tilt bed goes, and none of it is cheap to repair.

The practical answer on most orchards is to put the container on the headland or beside the shed rather than deep in the block, and to accept that the best spot for storage is usually the one nearest the hardstand you already have. If it genuinely has to go into the block, a crane truck can place it from the row end without driving in. Either way, tell us at the quote whether the block is netted, what the row spacing is, and whether the headland has been watered recently. We would rather send the right truck once than the wrong truck twice.

Worth knowing

After October 2022: siting on the floodplain

A lot of people around Shepparton and Mooroopna are storing things in containers now precisely because of what happened in October 2022, and it is worth being blunt about what a container does and does not do. It is a very good dry store. It is not a flood barrier. Water that reaches the door sill will get past the seals, and an empty container is a large sealed box that will float and move if the water gets deep enough. Anyone who tells you different is selling you something they should not.

What protects contents on a floodplain is height and position. Put it on the highest usable part of the block rather than the flattest. Keep it out of the overland flow path, because a lot of the damage in 2022 came from water running across properties rather than the river simply rising in place. If it is going to be a long-term store on low ground, talk to us about setting it up on blocks or a raised pad so the floor sits higher and the underside stays dry and ventilated — which is good practice on the valley's heavy soils anyway. And if what you are storing is genuinely irreplaceable, height beats every other decision you will make about it.

Common questions

Buying a container in Shepparton

Can you deliver a container into an orchard or between the rows?

We can get it onto the block, but rarely deep into the rows. Row spacing is set for a tractor and bin trailer, and a rigid truck cannot raise a tilt bed inside that width — and on netted blocks the netting and trellis wire are exactly where the bed wants to go. The usual answer is the headland at the end of the run or beside the existing shed hardstand, which is generally where you want it anyway. If it truly has to sit inside the block, a crane truck can place it from the row end without driving in. Tell us the row spacing and whether the block is netted when you enquire.

Is a shipping container worth having on a block that flooded in 2022?

Yes as a store, no as a barrier. Floodwater that reaches the door sill gets past the seals, and an empty container will float and shift if it gets deep enough — we will not pretend otherwise. What protects your contents is height and placement: the highest usable part of the block rather than the flattest, out of the overland flow path, and set up on blocks or a raised pad if it is a long-term store on low ground. Do that and it is an excellent dry store for the valley. Treat it as flood protection and it will let you down at the worst moment.

My driveway crosses an irrigation channel. Can a truck use it?

Maybe, and it is exactly the sort of thing we need to know before we book. Private crossings and farm bridges through the Goulburn–Murray network were mostly built for farm traffic, and channel banks are built ground that holds moisture and gives way at the shoulder under a heavy wheel. Send us a side-on photo of the crossing and tell us the heaviest thing that has been across it lately. Where there is any doubt we will use a crane truck from the nearest formed ground rather than risk your crossing.

How long from order to delivery in Shepparton?

Usually six to ten business days. Stock comes from the Melbourne terminals up the Goulburn Valley Highway, so the road leg is straightforward. The date moves on availability of your exact size and grade, whether a crane truck is needed, and ground conditions — in a wet week, or right after a bay has been watered, waiting two days beats making a mess of a paddock. Give us your date at the enquiry and we will tell you honestly whether it works.
