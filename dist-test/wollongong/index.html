Wollongong, NSW

Shipping containers Wollongong

There is not much flat ground in Wollongong and what there is has a house on it. The city is wedged onto a narrow shelf between the escarpment and the sea, which is lovely to live on and awkward to deliver to, because the streets follow contours rather than a grid and a great many houses sit below the level of the road they are addressed from. Add the grades a loaded truck has to negotiate to get here at all, and the Illawarra becomes a place where the access conversation happens before the price conversation, not after it.

How the drop usually goes

Getting to the Illawarra is itself part of the job. Mount Ousley is the main freight descent and it is a serious grade with brake-check requirements and arrester beds for a reason; Bulli Pass is not a road a long combination has any business on. Neither stops us, but both mean an Illawarra run is planned around traffic and time of day rather than squeezed in. The northern villages are the tightest part of the district. Thirroul, Austinmer, Coledale, Scarborough and Stanwell Park hang off the escarpment on a single coastal road with hairpins, one-lane pinch points, blind driveways and mature figs overhanging at four metres. A 20ft on a tilt-tray gets up there. A 40ft is a conversation to have before you order rather than after. The signature Wollongong access problem, though, is the house below the road. Right through Figtree, Mount Keira, Mount Pleasant, Balgownie and Woonona the block falls away from the kerb, the driveway pitches down hard and the usable yard is ten or twelve metres below the truck. A tilt-tray cannot place a container downhill onto that; a crane truck can lower it, and that is what we book. Underfoot, the escarpment foothills are sandstone shelf and slipping clay in roughly equal measure — there are recognised landslip zones on those slopes, and a heavy stationary load in the wrong spot on the wrong slope is not something to guess at. The coastal flats at Windang, Warilla and Shellharbour are sand. The Dapto, Koonawarra and Albion Park flats are low ground on Mullet Creek and the Macquarie Rivulet that hold water for days. And the escarpment squeezes serious rain out of an east coast low, so wet-weather rescheduling here is normal rather than an excuse.

Wollongong

The house is below the road

It is the most common Illawarra call we take. The address is fine, the street is fine, and the back yard is three metres below the driveway with a retaining wall between the two. A tilt-tray unloads by sliding a container rearwards down an inclined bed onto ground roughly level with the truck. It cannot place one at the bottom of a fall, and trying to reverse down a steep short drive with six metres of steel hanging past the rear axle ends with the bed grounding out on the kerb crest.

A crane truck solves it properly. It stands on the street, picks the container up and lowers it onto the lower ground, which turns an impossible job into a twenty-minute one. What it needs from you is information: how far down and how far across the container must travel, what is in between — roofline, pergola, palms, the neighbour's carport — and whether there are wires across the frontage. Reach falls away fast as the boom goes out, so eight metres over a fence and twenty metres over a two-storey house are different machines entirely. Stand at the gate and take three photos: down the drive, up at the lines, and from the yard looking back at the street.

On the ground

Rain, slope and where not to put it

The escarpment wrings rain out of anything coming in off the sea, and the Illawarra gets falls that flat country never sees. On a sloping block that changes where a container should sit. Water coming off the escarpment moves across blocks, not just down gutters, and a six-metre steel box parked square across an overland flow path will send that water somewhere the drainage was never designed for — usually against a wall or into the neighbour's yard.

The other half is what the container sits on. On a slope, build the low side up with stacked hardwood or set pads into the fall, rather than cutting a shelf into the high side. Cutting creates a trench that holds water against the bottom rail for the life of the unit. Building up keeps the underside open, keeps air moving and keeps all four corners at the same height, which is the only thing the frame actually cares about. Get that right and the doors keep swinging cleanly for decades. Get it wrong and the frame racks slowly, the doors start binding, and fixing it costs a great deal more than preventing it would have.

Worth knowing

Steel town storage, and whether to hire it

A lot of Illawarra container work is industrial and straightforward. A lock-up for tools and consumables on a leased lot at Unanderra or Kembla Grange, a store for spares that will not fit in the tenancy, a secure box for gear during a shutdown. Those deliveries are easy — the yards have hardstand, wide entries and room to work — so the only real decision is whether the container should be on your books or not.

Hire fits a lease with an end date, a project with a program, or a business that genuinely cannot say what it will need next winter. It also means the unit can be swapped for a bigger or smaller one if the job changes. Buying fits a permanent fixture on ground you control, and a bought 20ft holds value well here because there is real local demand for them second-hand. If it is going to live outside on the coast holding anything that must stay dry, ask for cargo-worthy — those units are inspected wind and watertight before loading. The grades page explains how the grades differ; tell us what is going inside and we will tell you which one you actually need.

Common questions

Buying a container in Wollongong

How do you get a container up to Thirroul, Austinmer or Stanwell Park?

Along the coast road, carefully, and usually as a 20ft rather than a 40ft. That stretch is narrow with hairpins, pinch points, blind driveways and low canopy, and the length of the vehicle matters more than the weight. We book those drops for a quiet part of the day rather than into weekend beach traffic. If your requirement is genuinely 40ft of length up there, tell us early — two 20ft placed end to end often delivers more usable space and a far simpler delivery.

My Figtree yard is well below the street. Is that a problem?

It is normal here and it is the reason we ask about the drop before booking a truck. A tilt-tray cannot place a container onto ground below the road, so those jobs go on a crane truck that lowers the unit into the yard from the street. It works well provided we know the horizontal distance, the height it must clear and whether there are power lines across the frontage. Photos from the driveway and from the yard looking back up are the fastest way to get a straight answer.

Will wet weather push my Illawarra delivery back?

Sometimes, and we would rather ring you than bog a truck on your block. The escarpment pulls very heavy rain out of an east coast low, and the flats around Dapto, Koonawarra and Albion Park hold water long after the surface looks dry, while the escarpment slopes go greasy. On a hardstand industrial site rain barely matters. On a soft or sloping residential block, waiting two days beats a set of ruts across your yard and a tractor to get the truck out.

How long is delivery to Wollongong?

Five to eight business days is the normal window. Illawarra containers come down from the Grafton depot on a scheduled coastal run, so it is a planned delivery rather than a next-day one, and a crane booking or a northern-suburbs address can add a day. If you have a fixed date, tell us at the enquiry and we will build the run around it instead of telling you afterwards it did not fit.
