Grade matters more than anything else in a container quote. Grade is what decides whether the box keeps water out. Anything sold as cargo-worthy is inspected wind and watertight before release, and new single-trip units — one loaded voyage, unmarked paint, rubbers that have barely weathered — are sealed as well. As-is is the cheap end and carries no watertight claim at all: expect a previous repair, a floor section that has gone soft, or a gasket that no longer pulls up hard against the frame. We list those faults and photograph them on request, before delivery, so nothing turns up as a surprise when the truck does. If what goes inside has to stay dry, start at cargo-worthy.

Delivery

The last thirty metres decides everything

Almost every delivery that goes wrong goes wrong for the same reason, and it is never the container. It is the run-in being too short, the pinch point being too narrow, a branch nobody measured, or ground that looked firm and gave way under four corner castings carrying a couple of tonne each.

So we ask about the site before we talk about price. Send three photographs — one from the street looking in, one down the approach, and one of the spot it has to land on — and you will be told which truck the job needs and whether it is a tilt-tray, a side loader or a crane job before anybody quotes a number.

Grades

Three grades cover nearly everything we move. The money between the top and the bottom of that range is usually larger than the gap between two yards quoting on the same grade, so settle the grade first and treat it as the only fair basis for comparing anybody's price.

New (single-trip)

Manufactured, packed once, sailed once, then stripped out at this end. Walls still true, floor unmarked, paint the original factory colour, rubbers that have seen almost no weather. Where the container will be on show — a shopfront, a display, the front of an acreage block — start here.

Cargo-worthy used

Still certified fit to carry freight at sea, which is the entire point of the grade. Scuffs, patches of surface rust and faded shipping-line livery come with it. Inspected wind and watertight before release. This is what most buyers should be looking at, and it is the bulk of what leaves the Cornubia yard.

As-is

Finished with sea service and priced on its faults. Cheapest by a distance, and perfectly serviceable for plenty of jobs — a locked store under an existing roof, a bund, the shell of a site office, the base of a build. Not sold watertight. The faults on the individual unit get described plainly and photographed on request before it goes anywhere near a truck.

Grades explained in full

New, used or refurbished

Three ways to buy the same box

The condition you buy decides more of the price than the size does, and it decides whether the unit is sold watertight.

New (Single-Trip)

A new container has been built, loaded once, carried one voyage and opened up at this end.

Best for: Conversions and fit-outs, display and customer-facing sites, long-term storage of anything that must stay dry, and buyers who would rather not think about doors or seals again for a decade.

Cargo-Worthy Used

Used covers a lot of ground, which is why it gets graded.

Best for: General storage, site security, farm and trade use, hire fleets, and anyone who wants the container to work rather than to look new.

Refurbished

A refurbished container is a used unit that has been repaired where it needed repairing and repainted in one colour.

Best for: Front-of-house storage, sites where several containers stand together and ought to match, rental and hire fleets, and anyone who wants a used price with a presentable finish.

Common questions

The things people ring and ask

Can I come and look at a container before I buy it?

Yes, and on used stock we would rather you did. The yard is at 51–55 Bromley Street, Cornubia QLD 4130, about half an hour south-east of the Brisbane CBD off the M1. Ring first so somebody is there and the units you want are accessible, then walk around them, open the doors and look down the floor yourself. If you are nowhere near Brisbane, ask for photographs of the actual unit on request and we will send them before delivery — corners, door end, roof and floor.

Do you deliver outside Queensland?

Yes. We deliver into every state and territory, and stock sits in depots at Cornubia, Grafton, Gympie, Rockhampton, Mackay, Townsville, Cairns, Darwin and Fremantle, so most addresses are a run from somewhere reasonably close rather than a haul across the continent. Remote runs are honest about timing — some of them wait on a truck already heading that way, and we will tell you that up front rather than after you have paid.

What grade should I buy?

Cargo-worthy used is the right answer for most people — a working container still certified fit for sea freight, and checked wind and watertight before it leaves. New single-trip is the buy when the container will be looked at or converted. As-is is cheapest and is not sold watertight; it suits a lock-up under cover or a base for a build. The grades page walks through all three properly.

What does delivery cost?

It depends on the distance and, more than most people expect, on the access at your end. A flat industrial site with a wide gate and a tight residential driveway on a hill are different jobs even when they are the same distance from the depot. We quote delivery together with the container so there is one number in front of you rather than a price with a question mark after it. One phone call and a couple of photos of the entry usually settles it.

How quickly can I get one?

A standard 20ft going somewhere with reasonable access around south-east Queensland is usually a few business days. Interstate capitals run longer, remote runs longer again, and the wet season closes unsealed roads across the north and the west for weeks at a time. Tell us the date you actually need it and you will get an honest answer about whether it is achievable, not an optimistic one.

Do I need council approval to put one on my block?

It depends entirely on your council, how long it is staying and what you are using it for. Plenty of shires treat a container as a temporary or ancillary structure needing no approval; others want a siting application, particularly if it is visible from the street or staying permanently, and newer estates often carry a covenant stricter than the council position. It is a short phone call to your own council and worth making before delivery day rather than after.
