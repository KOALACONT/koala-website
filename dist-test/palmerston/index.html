Getting a truck in

Palmerston was built as a planned satellite city from the 1980s, and it still reads that way from a truck cab. The older suburbs such as Gray, Driver, Moulden and Woodroffe were laid out around loop roads and cul-de-sacs, with shaded streets and houses set back behind mature gardens. Big trees over the kerb and driveways that run beside the house are the usual constraints there, and most lots can take a 20ft if the truck can line up. Further out, in Rosebery, Bakewell, Durack and Farrar, the blocks are a little tighter and the houses sit closer together. The eastern estates are the newest and change fastest: some streets still have lots waiting to be built on, kerbs and crossovers are not always finished, and the ground beside a finished house can be freshly placed fill. Townhouse and unit complexes are their own category. They often have a gated entry, a narrow internal driveway with speed humps, carports with low roofs, and very little open ground, so a container usually goes in a visitor bay or a common area with the body corporate's agreement, or not at all. Yarrawonga is straightforward, with industrial lots, roller doors, room to turn and hardstand that suits a truck. Ground across Palmerston is mostly laterite gravel that carries weight well once compacted, but freshly filled lots and the lower ground near the creeks and lagoons stay soft through the wet. Photos of the street, the approach and the spot, plus the gate code and any access instructions, let us plan the whole job before the truck leaves the depot.

On the ground

Postings, rentals and body corporates

Palmerston has a large defence community, and postings create a very particular storage need. A family arrives before a house is ready, or leaves a house before the removalist can take everything, or needs somewhere to keep the boat and the camper while living in something smaller for a while. A container on hire covers the gap and goes away when it is no longer needed. If you are on a posting, check what your relocation arrangements already cover before you order, and tell us how long you expect to need it. Hire suits a defined gap; buying suits storage that will go with you to the next property.

Renters and townhouse owners have extra people to ask. A tenant needs the landlord's permission before a container goes on a rental property, and it pays to get that in writing. In a townhouse or unit complex, the body corporate decides whether a container can sit on common property or a visitor bay, and for how long. The City of Palmerston has its own rules for containers on residential land, and whether a container that is staying needs a building permit or an engineered tie-down in this wind region is a question for a building certifier. None of that is a reason not to order, but it should be settled before a truck turns up.

The newer eastern estates bring a different kind of paperwork. Zuccoli, Johnston and the streets beyond them are full of lots under construction, and a container on a building site sits there with the builder's agreement, placed where it will not get in the way of the slab, the frame or the deliveries. Estate guidelines may also say something about outbuildings and what can be seen from the street. On a finished lot, remember that the fill beside a new house has not had many wet seasons to settle, so a container should go on well-bedded pads rather than straight onto new turf.

Worth knowing

Build-up afternoons and a steel box in the sun

From around October the build-up brings heavy, humid heat and afternoon storms with a lot of lightning, then the monsoon arrives and the rain settles in. A steel container sitting in full sun through that season gets very hot inside, and when a storm cools the air suddenly, moisture condenses on the inside of the roof and walls. That is hard on anything with electronics, adhesives or batteries, on paper and fabric, and on furniture that has come up from a drier climate. Ventilation is the first answer, with vents at both ends and whirlybirds if the contents are sensitive. Shade helps a great deal, whether from trees or a shade structure, as long as any structure has the approvals it needs.

Siting matters too. Put the container on the highest practical part of the lot, clear of the path water takes when a storm dumps on the suburb, and on pads rather than directly on the ground so air moves underneath and the underside dries after each downpour. Monsoon rain from above stays outside; stormwater pooling around the base is another matter, because it seeps past the gaskets, and deep enough water can move an unladen unit. Keep the roof clear of leaves and mango litter so water runs off instead of sitting in a corner. Common questions

Buying a container in Palmerston

Can I look at a container before it is delivered to Palmerston?

Yes. Our Darwin stock is held at a depot in the Berrimah area, a short drive from Palmerston, and inspection there is by arrangement. Ring first so the unit you want is pulled out where you can open it and look inside properly. If a visit does not suit, ask for photos of the actual container on request and we will send them before delivery, showing the doors, floor, roof and corners.

Can I put a container on a rental property or in a townhouse complex?

Only with the right permission. On a rental you need the landlord's agreement, ideally in writing. In a townhouse or unit complex the body corporate decides whether a container may sit on common property or in a visitor bay, and for how long. Check the City of Palmerston rules as well. Once that is sorted, send photos of the entry, the internal driveway and the spot, because gated complexes often have low carports and tight turns.

Does a container in Palmerston need to be tied down?

It depends on how long it stays and what it is used for. Palmerston is in a cyclone wind region, and a container that becomes a permanent fixture may need a building permit and an engineered tie-down. That is a question for a building certifier and the City of Palmerston, not for us, and it is worth asking before the unit arrives. An empty container is lighter than most people think, so do not assume it will stay put in a big blow.

Is hire or buying better for a defence posting?

Hire usually suits a posting with a known end date, such as a gap between houses or a few months in temporary accommodation. The container goes when you do. Buying makes more sense if you want long-term storage on a property you own, or if the container will go with you. Check what your relocation arrangements already cover, tell us how long you need it for, and we will price both.

Can you deliver to a business in Yarrawonga?

Yes, and it is usually one of the simpler jobs in Palmerston. Yarrawonga lots are built for trucks, with hardstand, wide gates and room to turn. Tell us whether a forklift is on site, where the container should go, and whether anything overhead, such as an awning, a gantry or a power line, will affect a tilt-tray or crane. For a 40ft, a side loader or crane truck is often the better choice, and we will confirm which one fits once we see photos.
