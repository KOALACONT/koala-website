Gympie, QLD

Shipping containers Gympie

We hold stock in Gympie, so a container for a Gympie address does not have to start its life on a truck three hundred kilometres south. That is worth more than it sounds when you want one this week. The awkward part is the town. Gympie was laid out around gold rather than around trucks, so the streets follow the gullies and the ridges instead of a grid, and a good half of the residential blocks fall away hard from the kerb. Add the Mary and where you put the thing matters as much as which one you buy.

Where they turn up around here

Around Gympie we deliver containers for hay, fencing wire and machinery parts on the beef and dairy country through the Mary Valley, dry storage for the macadamia and small crop growers around Amamoor, Kandanga and Imbil, timber and tool storage for the forestry and harvesting contractors working out of Curra, Glastonbury and Wolvi, boats, crab pots and dive gear at Tin Can Bay and Rainbow Beach where the garage gave up years ago, site stores on the new builds at Southside and Jones Hill, and household storage while a Queenslander at Monkland or One Mile gets restumped and reclad.

What has to be right before the truck books

Gympie divides into ridge and river, and the two want different trucks. The high ground — Victory Heights, Jones Hill, The Palms, the top end of Monkland — is where most of the housing sits, and it got that way because the flats were never a safe bet. Those streets were pushed through gullies and the driveways come off them at a pitch a car takes without noticing and a loaded tilt-tray does not. Where a drive drops sharply at the kerb, the back of the bed touches bitumen before the container has slid clear. Where it crests halfway up, the same thing happens in the middle instead of at the start. On a lot of those blocks the sensible answer is to lift over the fence from the street rather than try to back in. The old town on the flat is the opposite complaint: short frontages, side accesses set out long before anyone owned a car, and fifty years of fig and camphor laurel hanging over the road at exactly truck height. Out through the Mary Valley it turns rural again — Kandanga, Amamoor, Imbil, Widgee, Glastonbury — with unsealed drives that have a crown and table drains, private grids nobody can put a rating to, and turning space at the far end that needs to be real rather than hopeful. Ground varies enormously over short distances here. Down on the flats it is river silt, firm in September and treacherous a week into February. Up on the ridges it is shallow soil over rock, which carries anything you like and is almost never level. Neither stops a delivery, but both change how the unit gets bedded down. Tell us the street, what the last hundred metres looks like and whether it is sealed, and we will have the truck and the size settled before anything is booked.

Grade moves the price more than size does, and it is what decides whether a unit is sold watertight — the full rundown is on the grades page.

Stock on the ground in Gympie

We keep containers at a depot in Gympie, which is not something most sellers quoting this postcode can say. It changes two things. The first is timing — a unit already standing in town can be on your block inside a day or two rather than waiting on a slot in a run coming up the Bruce. The second is that you can lay eyes on it. Inspection is by arrangement rather than a walk-in, because the yard is not set up or staffed for the public, so ring and we will book a time and have someone there. What we will not do is print an address on a website and let you drive to a locked gate on a Saturday.

What is standing locally moves from week to week, so the honest answer on grade and size is that it depends. If you want something specific — a high cube instead of a standard, a double-door unit, a particular grade — say so when you ring. Sometimes it is already in Gympie. Sometimes it comes up on the next run and adds a few days to the date. You will be told which of those two it is, in plain terms, at the quote. If you cannot get to an inspection, ask for photos of the actual unit on request and we will send them through before delivery. Those are photos of the unit that is coming to you rather than a stock shot of something similar — corners, door end, roof and the length of the floor. It takes us two minutes and it saves a difficult conversation on the morning the truck arrives.

On the ground

The Mary, and where the box ought to sit

Gympie has been through more floods than anyone here needs reminding of, and February 2022 put water through parts of town that had stayed dry for a generation. So it is worth being blunt about what a container does. It keeps rain off from above and it locks. It is not a barrier against rising water. Once the level reaches the door sill, water gets past the seals and into the box, and an empty container carries enough trapped air to lift off the ground and drift. If a seller tells you different, they are either guessing or not being straight with you. None of that makes a container a poor store in this town. It makes it one that has to be sited by somebody thinking about water rather than about how far it is from the back door, which is the decision most people get wrong.

What actually protects your gear is height and position. Pick the highest ground you can genuinely use, even if it means a longer walk from the back door with an armful of gear. Stay off the line water takes when it runs across the block, because around here a gully going hard does as much harm as the river coming up. If it has to live on low ground, we can set it up on a raised pad or on blocks so the floor sits well clear and the underside stays ventilated, and we can talk about anchoring so it does not travel if it ever gets buoyant. And think about what goes where inside it. Shelving at chest height is worth a great deal more than floor space for anything you would be upset to lose.

Worth knowing

When lifting beats backing in

A crane delivery gets treated as the expensive option, and on a Gympie ridge block it is often the cheap one, because it goes in first time. A tilt-tray needs a straight, reasonably flat run of nine or ten metres for a 20ft, and it needs the driveway to stay at roughly the same grade the whole way. Half the streets off Tozer Street and up around Victory Heights cannot offer that. A crane truck stands on the road, lifts the unit over the fence or the carport and sets it exactly where you want it, which on a narrow block is also the only way to get it hard against a boundary. What decides whether that works is reach and what is in the way: how far the container has to travel horizontally, the height of the roofline or the trees it clears, whether anything is strung overhead at the front of the property, and whether there is enough road width to get the outriggers down without closing it off. Two photos answer most of that — one from the middle of the road looking at the house, one from the spot looking back toward the street.

Common questions

Buying a container in Gympie

Do you actually have containers in Gympie, or do they come from Brisbane?

We hold stock at a depot in Gympie. Not every size and grade is standing there every week, and where we do not have what you want locally it comes up from our Cornubia yard on the next run, but a straightforward 20ft is usually available in town. You can come and look at one, though it has to be arranged first — there is no counter and nobody waiting at the gate. We do not print that address anywhere, because an unannounced visit just means a locked fence and a wasted trip.

My driveway drops away hard from the road at Victory Heights. Can you still deliver?

Usually, but probably not on a tilt-tray. Where the drive pitches down steeply off the kerb, the rear of the bed grounds on the bitumen before the container has cleared it, and no amount of care changes that. The usual fix is a crane truck standing on the road and lifting the unit over the fence, or dropping a size where a 40ft was the original idea. Stand at the kerb, take one picture up the drive and one looking back down it, and we can normally settle it over the phone.

Is a container a sensible store on a flood-prone Gympie block?

Yes, provided you treat it as a lock-up rather than a barrier. Once water reaches the door sill it works past the seals, and an empty unit carries enough trapped air to lift and drift. Elevation is what actually saves the contents. Choose height over convenience when you pick the spot, keep the unit off the line water takes when it runs across the block, and think about standing it on blocks or a built pad so the floor sits above the surface. Set up that way it earns its keep; bought as flood protection it will disappoint you at the worst possible moment.

Can you get a container down a Mary Valley property driveway?

Regularly, and most of them are fine when the surface is dry and bound. What stops us is a greasy formed-earth drive after rain, a table drain a wheel can drop into, a private grid nobody can put a load rating to, and no room to line up straight at the far end. Where any of that applies, the fallback is to stand the container on firm ground near the road so you can drag it in with the tractor in your own time, or to send a crane truck that can park once and reach further than a tilt could. Say so at the booking rather than at the front gate.
