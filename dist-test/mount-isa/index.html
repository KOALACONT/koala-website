Mount Isa, QLD

Shipping containers Mount Isa

This is the longest haul on our list and there is no point dressing it up. Stock for Mount Isa comes from our Townsville depot and runs the better part of nine hundred kilometres west on the Flinders and the Barkly. It goes when freight is heading that way, which is usually inside a fortnight and occasionally quicker. Once it arrives you get a different set of problems to anywhere else we deliver: rock instead of soil, and heat that changes what you can sensibly keep inside a steel box.

Common uses in and around town

Around Mount Isa we deliver containers for spares, tooling and consumables for the workshops and contracting businesses servicing the mines, somewhere sealed to keep red dust out of filters, hose, fittings and rigging in yards where everything wears out fast, secure lock-ups for the fabrication and engineering shops around the industrial area, station storage for bore spares, poly fittings, fencing and animal health lines on the country out toward Camooweal, Dajarra and the Cloncurry side, gear storage for the schools, clubs and community groups in town, and household storage on the rocky residential blocks where a shed would need serious excavation.

How the drop usually goes

Mount Isa is built on and around rock, and that is the first thing that separates it from anywhere else on this list. Blocks step up hillsides, driveways are steep and short, retaining walls and terracing are everywhere, and the ground behind the house is frequently a metre or two above the street. The good news is that rock carries a container beautifully and nothing sinks. The bad news is that almost nothing is level, and a spot that will take a container often cannot be reached by a truck with the bed up. On the steeper residential blocks a crane lift from the street is regularly the only sensible answer, and what decides it is straightforward enough: the horizontal distance from where the truck can stand to where the container has to land, the height of everything between the two, any services strung across the frontage, and whether the street gives the outriggers firm ground and enough width. In town the industrial and commercial frontages are generous and the delivery is straightforward. Out of town the constraints change again. The Barkly west toward Camooweal and the roads south toward Dajarra and Boulia carry road trains, and station approaches off the bitumen can be long, rocky and rough, with grids of unknown rating and floodways that reshape after every wet. Bulldust holes on a well-used track look like road and are not. And in a wet season the creeks and floodways out here come up fast and go down fast, but while they are up nothing crosses. Tell us whether we are delivering to a street, a yard or a property, and if it is a property, how far off the bitumen and what the surface is.

Heat, and what it does to what is inside

A closed steel container in a Mount Isa summer builds an internal temperature that surprises people the first time they open the doors in the afternoon. That is not a fault and it is not something a better container fixes. It is physics, and it is worth designing around before the unit leaves our yard rather than discovering it in November. Vents are the first and cheapest step, because they let hot air move and give trapped moisture somewhere to go. A lighter roof and wall colour makes a real difference too, and where the block allows it, siting the container so it picks up some afternoon shade is worth more than any accessory. None of that is exotic. It is just worth deciding before the truck leaves the coast rather than after.

The second half of it is what you put inside. Aerosols, batteries, fuels, adhesives, some chemicals and anything with a storage temperature printed on the label are all worth a second thought before they live in a closed container through a Mount Isa February. Rubber and plastics age faster. Anything sealed with air in it expands. None of that makes a container the wrong choice, it just means the specification should match the job. Tell us what is going in at the quote and we will talk through vents, colour and placement rather than sending you a bare box and letting you work it out. Where the contents genuinely cannot take the heat, the honest answer is that a container is a storeroom and not a cool room, and it should be specified with that understood.

On the ground

Rock, not soil

Most of what gets written about placing containers is about soft ground, and almost none of it applies here. Around Mount Isa the problem is the opposite: the ground will hold anything, but it is rarely flat and you cannot dig it out with a shovel. That changes the approach to levelling. Instead of building a pad and worrying about settlement, you are packing the low corners up to meet the high ones, usually with stacked hardwood or with pads bedded onto the rock, until all four corner castings sit at the same height. That is what keeps the frame square and the doors swinging cleanly, and it matters just as much here as it does on black soil, only for a different reason.

The other rock-country consideration is the approach. A steep short driveway onto a terraced block will ground out a tilt bed before the container is clear, and a retaining wall means the only usable ground is above or below where the truck can stand. That is work for a crane rather than a tilt bed, and it is far better established before the day than on it. Three photos answer nearly all of it: one from the middle of the street looking at the property, one looking up at whatever is overhead, and one of the spot itself with a bit of the surroundings in frame. Include the retaining wall if there is one, because the height of the wall often decides the lift rather than the distance across the block.

Worth knowing

Ordering once, nine hundred kilometres out

The distance changes how much care goes into the order. When a container has run from Townsville out through Hughenden, Julia Creek and Cloncurry, a change of mind at the far end is not a swap, it is another freight movement. So we spend longer on a Mount Isa order than we would on a coastal one, and it is worth the phone call. It is also why we would rather over-ask at the quote. The questions that sound fussy on the phone, such as how wide the gate is, how much fall is on the pad and what is going inside, are the same questions that stop a container turning up in a configuration that does not suit the job. Ten minutes now beats a fortnight later.

Size first. A 20ft is the workhorse and it is far easier onto a rocky, terraced or steep block than a 40ft. Where you want machinery length, a 40ft suits a flat commercial yard with a straight approach. Two 20ft can go in two different places, which on a stepped block is often more useful than one long unit. Then fit-out. A lockbox over the handle, vents, shelving, load-restraint points, a side door, a coat of paint. Every one of those is an hour's work at the depot and a genuine exercise once the container is standing in a yard out on the Barkly. Common questions

Buying a container in Mount Isa

How long does it take to get a container to Mount Isa?

One to three weeks is the honest range. Stock comes from our Townsville depot and travels the better part of nine hundred kilometres west on the Flinders and the Barkly through Hughenden, Julia Creek and Cloncurry, so it goes when freight is running that way. Sometimes that is quick. If you need a firm date, tell us at the enquiry and we will tell you whether the next scheduled run will hold it or whether it needs a dedicated trip.

Does the heat damage what I store in a container here?

It can, and it is worth planning for. A closed steel box builds serious internal temperature through a Mount Isa afternoon. Aerosols, batteries, fuels, adhesives, some chemicals and anything with a storage temperature on the label all deserve a second thought. Vents fitted before the container travels let hot air move and give moisture somewhere to go, a lighter roof colour helps, and siting the unit where it gets afternoon shade helps most of all. Tell us what is going inside and we will spec it properly.

My Mount Isa block is steep and rocky. Can you still place a container?

Usually yes, but often with a crane rather than a tilt-tray. Short steep driveways ground a tilt bed out before the container is clear, and on a terraced block the only usable ground is frequently above or below where the truck can stand. A crane lift off the street handles both. The deciding factors are how far the boom has to reach, what it has to clear on the way, any services over the frontage, and firm level ground wide enough for the outriggers. Send a photo from the street, one looking up at the lines and one of the spot itself.

How do you level a container on rock?

By packing the low corners up rather than digging the high side down, since you are not excavating Mount Isa rock with a shovel. Stacked hardwood or pads bedded onto the rock, built up until all four corner castings sit at the same height, keeps the frame square and the doors swinging properly. Put the door end on the high side wherever there is a choice. The good news is that once it is set on rock it stays where you put it, which is more than can be said for black soil country.
