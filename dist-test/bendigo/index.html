Bendigo, VIC

Shipping containers Bendigo

Bendigo's gold was reef gold, so the workings here went down rather than along, and the legacy is thousands of capped, filled and quietly forgotten shafts under suburbs people live in now. Above them the soil is thin — quartz gravel and ironbark country, with rock inside a spade's depth in a good half of the yards we visit. Between those two facts sits most of what makes a Bendigo delivery interesting. You are not hunting for the flattest spot on the block. You are hunting for the spot that will still be at that level in five years.

What matters locally, first.

What the containers do here

Around Bendigo we deliver containers for storage while an Eaglehawk or Golden Square miner's cottage gets restumped and reroofed, dry lock-up on the newer blocks at Strathfieldsaye, Huntly and Maiden Gully, hay, feed and fencing storage on the cropping and grazing country north through Marong, Raywood and Elmore, tool and plant lock-up for the engineering trades around the East Bendigo industrial land, barrel, netting and harvest gear for growers out towards Heathcote, and secure off-site storage for households on the box-ironbark fringe clearing the yard back before summer.

What the delivery actually involves

The single most useful thing to know before a Bendigo delivery is what the ground is made of at the exact spot you have chosen, because it changes across a single block here. Through Long Gully, California Gully, Eaglehawk and Ironbark you are often on or beside old mullock — the crushed rock heaped out of the workings — which was tipped rather than compacted and has been quietly rearranged by every wet winter since. Thirty metres away you can hit reef rock so close to the surface that a fence post is a two-hour job. Neither one is a problem once we know about it; the trouble comes from assuming a lawn is a lawn. In the older suburbs the streets add their own complications. Quarry Hill, Kennington, Ironbark and the streets behind View Street have short steep drives off a crowned road, low verandah lines, and enormous old peppercorns and elms that a tilt bed cannot get under. That is a crane truck job more often here than in most towns, and it is worth deciding at the quote. The estates east and north — Strathfieldsaye, Junortoun, Huntly, Epsom, Maiden Gully — bring the usual growth-corridor combination of narrow carriageways, kerbside service pits that will not carry a loaded rear axle and turning heads a rigid truck has to reverse out of. On the bush blocks around Maiden Gully, Junortoun and Mandurang the constraint is different again: gravel tracks with a canopy of ironbark over them, gates on a bend, and a track that was formed for a ute. Measure the narrowest point and the lowest branch and send us the numbers. We would rather know than turn a truck around at your gate.

Bendigo

Up the Calder, and how we book it

There is no yard of ours in Victoria, so a Bendigo container is released in Melbourne and comes up the Calder by road. That is a clean run and it is not usually what sets your date. What sets your date is whether the exact size and grade you want is available in Melbourne on the day you commit, and whether the job needs a crane truck. Between those, most Bendigo deliveries land somewhere in the five to nine business day range.

The practical advice is to enquire earlier than feels necessary and to tell us what the container is actually for. A 20ft going onto a hardstand at an East Bendigo workshop is a different job from a 40ft going down a gravel track at Mandurang, and the second one wants a conversation, a couple of photos and possibly a different truck. If you have a fixed date — a settlement, a shed going up, a house being packed — say so up front. We will build the run around it or tell you straight away that it will not fit, which is more useful than finding out on the day.

On the ground

Shafts, mullock and thin soil over reef

A container is not like a shed. Almost all of its weight travels through the four corner castings, so instead of a broad even load you are putting four concentrated point loads into the ground. In most towns that is a footnote. In Bendigo it is the main event, because the district is riddled with old shafts, costeans and filled workings, plenty of them from an era with no records worth the name, and because the fill on a lot of older blocks is mullock rather than soil.

What we do about it is simple. If there is any known history on the block — subsidence, a hole that has opened before, a hollow-sounding patch, a neighbour's story — tell us at the quote. We will pick the firmest ground rather than the flattest, spread the corner loads onto larger pads or sleepers, and where there is real doubt suggest a 20ft over a 40ft so the whole footprint sits inside ground we are comfortable with. Thin soil over rock is the easier version of the same problem: you cannot dig a level pad, so you shim up to the high corner with timber instead. Either way it costs nothing to plan and a great deal to fix.

Worth knowing

Ironbark fringe: clearance, turning and summer

The box-ironbark blocks that ring Bendigo are some of the best places in the district to live and some of the fiddliest to deliver to. Tracks were formed for a ute, gates sit on a bend rather than a straight, and the canopy hangs low enough that a tilt bed at full extension will meet a branch before it meets the ground. We need three numbers from you: the narrowest point on the track, the lowest branch over it, and how far it is from the last piece of formed road. A photo standing at the gate looking in tells us more than any of them.

Placement on those blocks is worth thinking about beyond the delivery. A container on a bush block usually ends up holding the things people would hate to lose, so keep it accessible on a wet day, keep it clear of the drip line of big trees where limbs come down, keep the ground around it clear rather than letting scrub grow up hard against the doors, and think about whether a truck could get back to it in a hurry if you ever needed to move it. And be realistic about what a steel box is: it keeps rain off from above and it keeps people out. It is not a fire refuge and we will not sell it to you as one.

Common questions

Buying a container in Bendigo

Are old mine shafts really a concern for a container in Bendigo?

They are worth asking about, yes. A container puts nearly all of its weight through four corner castings, which is four point loads rather than a spread one, and Bendigo's reef workings are extensive, deep and only partly recorded. Most blocks are completely fine. If yours has a history of subsidence, a soft patch that keeps returning, or is on old mullock fill through Long Gully or California Gully, tell us at the quote. We will site it on the firmest ground, spread the corners onto larger pads, and if there is genuine doubt suggest a shorter unit so the footprint stays where we are confident.

How quickly can you deliver to Bendigo?

Usually five to nine business days. Containers come from the Melbourne terminals up the Calder, so the road leg is short and predictable. The variables are stock — whether your exact size and grade is available in Melbourne when you commit — and whether the site needs a crane truck rather than a tilt-tray, which wants a few days' notice. If you have a hard date, tell us at the enquiry and we will either build the run around it or say straight away that it cannot be done.

Can you deliver onto a bush block at Maiden Gully, Junortoun or Mandurang?

Usually, but it needs planning. Send us the narrowest point on the track, the lowest overhanging branch, and a photo taken at the gate looking in. Ironbark canopy is the thing that catches people out — a tilt-tray needs serious height clearance while the bed is up, and a track that a ute clears easily can stop a truck dead. Where the canopy or the turning space will not work, a crane truck placing the container from the nearest formed hardstand is often the answer instead.

Do I need approval to put a container on my block in Bendigo?

That depends on your zoning, whether the container is permanent or temporary, and whether the property is in a heritage precinct — and the older parts of central Bendigo and Eaglehawk have plenty of those. We are not in a position to advise on your particular block, so check with the City of Greater Bendigo before you order, especially for anything visible from the street or intended to stay long term. It is a short phone call and it is a much better time to have the conversation than after a container is sitting there.
