Side Opening Shipping Containers

In short

What you are getting

  • Doors along the whole of one side, plus the standard pair at the end
  • New in 20ft and 40ft; used side openers surface rarely and sell fast
  • Turns the container from a tunnel into a shelf you can work from outside
  • Needs clear, level ground alongside the doors, so where the container can stand changes
  • Considerably dearer than a general purpose unit, and worth every cent on the right job

Access is the only reason to pay the difference. In an ordinary container whatever you want is behind whatever went in first, and in practice that means dragging half the load onto the ground to reach it. A side opener does away with that completely. For a workshop, a parts store, a hire yard or anywhere stock cycles quickly, it changes the way the container gets used every day of the week.

Siting works differently and this is where people come unstuck. The leaves swing outwards across the full length, so allow about three metres of clear, flat standing along that side, and settle which way the container faces before the truck arrives. Spinning one around afterwards is another delivery. Decide which side the doors face, say so when you order, and the driver will land it that way round.

They are heavier and structurally more complicated than a plain box, so they cost more and there are simply fewer of them in circulation. Used examples do exist but they are uncommon — if one is wanted, say so early and we will keep an eye out rather than wait for one to appear.

Grade matters more than anything else in a container quote. Grade is what decides whether the box keeps water out. Anything sold as cargo-worthy is inspected wind and watertight before release, and new single-trip units — one loaded voyage, unmarked paint, rubbers that have barely weathered — are sealed as well. As-is is the cheap end and carries no watertight claim at all: expect a previous repair, a floor section that has gone soft, or a gasket that no longer pulls up hard against the frame. We list those faults and photograph them on request, before delivery, so nothing turns up as a surprise when the truck does. If what goes inside has to stay dry, start at cargo-worthy.

Common questions

About side opening containers

What is a side opening shipping container?

A container with doors running the full length of one side on top of the usual pair at the end. Nothing ends up buried behind anything else, every pallet position is reachable from outside, and a forklift can pull any one of them without shifting the rest.

What sizes do side opening containers come in?

Generally 20ft and 40ft, and in some configurations 10ft. Availability moves week to week, especially on used stock. Ring and ask what is actually standing on the ground rather than working off a list.

Is a side opening container watertight?

In cargo-worthy grade or better, yes — every cargo-worthy unit is checked wind and watertight before it leaves us. As-is units are cheaper again and are not sold watertight. Grade decides this, not configuration, and it is worth reading the grades page before you choose.

How much more does a side opening container cost?

It depends on the configuration and on what is available. Some cost only a little more than a standard general purpose unit; side opening and dangerous goods units cost substantially more because they are structurally different containers, not modified ones.
