The case for it

Where a 40ft earns its place

  • Often only a little more than a used twenty for about twice the volume on current quotes. Grade and the delivery run move that, so ask for both figures rather than assuming.
  • One truck instead of two, one lot of cartage instead of two, one prepared footing instead of two.
  • Mates and stacks neatly with a second forty, which is what makes the twin-container covered bay such a common build.

What actually goes in one

A big house plus the contents of the shed, or a proper workshop with an aisle down the centre to work in. Frequently bought two at a time and stood parallel, with a roof spanning the gap to make a covered bay.

The bit people wish they had known

Everything hinges on access at this length. Reckon on 30 metres of straight approach, 4 metres of width at the tightest point, nothing hanging over any of it, and ground firm enough that four corner castings will not sink into it. A side loader manages tighter ground than a tilt-tray; on most side loaders the container comes off the driver's side of the trailer, so the direction of approach matters and the carrier confirms it on the day. Grade matters more than anything else in a container quote. Grade is what decides whether the box keeps water out. Anything sold as cargo-worthy is inspected wind and watertight before release — for storage use, which is separate from the CSC certification an export job needs — and new single-trip units, one loaded voyage with rubbers that have barely weathered, are sealed as well. As-is is the cheap end and carries no watertight claim at all: expect a previous repair, a floor section that has gone soft, or a gasket that no longer pulls up hard against the frame. We list those faults and photograph them on request, before delivery, so nothing turns up as a surprise when the truck does. If what goes inside has to stay dry, start at cargo-worthy.

Questions

The 40ft, answered

What are the dimensions of a 40ft shipping container?

Outside, 12.19m × 2.44m × 2.59m. Inside, 12.03m × 2.35m × 2.39m, which gives 67.7m³ of usable room, through a door opening of 2.34m × 2.28m, at an empty weight of approx 3,800 kg. Those are ISO figures and they hold whoever built the box, so there is no point shopping on them. What does differ from one 40ft to the next is how sound the floor is and how hard the doors pull up against the seal, and that is a grade question rather than a dimensions one.

What fits in a 40ft container?

A big house plus the contents of the shed, or a proper workshop with an aisle down the centre to work in. Frequently bought two at a time and stood parallel, with a roof spanning the gap to make a covered bay.

How much does a 40ft shipping container cost?

It is priced on the grade — new single-trip, cargo-worthy used or as-is — and quoted for the exact unit with delivery to your address, in AUD ex GST. Where any individual 40ft lands comes down to its condition, to what is physically standing in the yard the week you ring, and to which yard it has to be released from. Cartage is worked out per address and quoted alongside the box rather than published, because the access at the far end shifts it as much as the distance does.

What does a 40ft container need for delivery?

Reckon on 30 metres of straight approach, 4 metres of width at the tightest point, and nothing overhanging any part of the run. A side loader copes with a good deal less room than a tilt-tray, but on most side loaders the container comes off the driver's side of the trailer — so the direction the truck can come in from tends to dictate which side of the block the box can end up on. The site and the carrier decide which truck it is; settle that well before delivery day.

Can I hire a 40ft rather than buy one?

Yes. Hire earns its keep when the container has a finish date on it — a build, a fit-out, a harvest, a rebuild after storm damage. If it is still going to be sitting there in two years, buying is nearly always the cheaper end of the deal by a wide margin. Give us the period and both numbers get run for you.
